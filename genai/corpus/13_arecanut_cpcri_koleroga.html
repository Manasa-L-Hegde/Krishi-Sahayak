Section: Chapter 4: Fruit Rot (Koleroga or Mahali) caused by Phytophthora meadii McRae

1. Causal Organism and Economic Impact

Fruit rot disease, locally known as Koleroga or Mahali, is caused by the oomycete pathogen Phytophthora meadii (syn. Phytophthora arecae). It is the most destructive disease of arecanut in high-rainfall tracts of the Western Ghats, coastal Karnataka, Kerala, and parts of Tamil Nadu and Meghalaya. Under continuous heavy rainfall, high atmospheric relative humidity (>90%), and temperatures ranging between 20°C and 24°C during the South-West monsoon, the disease spreads rapidly by rain-splash, resulting in heavy premature shedding of green nuts and yield losses ranging from 10% to 90%.

2. Chemical Control and Bordeaux Mixture Application Schedule

The standard and most effective prophylactic control measure recommended by ICAR-CPCRI is spraying 1% neutral Bordeaux mixture on the bunches.

  • Product and Concentration: 1% Bordeaux mixture prepared by dissolving 1 kg copper sulphate (CuSO4·5H2O) and 1 kg freshly slaked lime [calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2] in 100 litres of water. The mixture must be strictly neutral in reaction (pH 7.0), tested using a clean iron knife blade or litmus paper before spraying.
  • First Spray (Pre-Monsoon): Prophylactic spraying of 1% Bordeaux mixture on all arecanut bunches thoroughly just before the onset of the regular south-west monsoon showers (late May or first week of June).
  • Second Spray (Inter-Monsoon): A second spray of 1% Bordeaux mixture must be administered 40 to 45 days after the first spray during a clear break or lull in the monsoon rainfall.
  • Third Spray: If the monsoon is prolonged or continuous heavy rainfall prevails into late August or September, a third spray of 1% Bordeaux mixture becomes necessary to protect late-developing bunches.

3. Adhesives and Stickers

Because heavy monsoon rains tend to wash off copper deposits from the smooth cuticle of arecanut fruits, the addition of a suitable agricultural adhesive or wetting agent is advised:

Advisories recommend adding an approved commercial sticker-cum-spreader at the rate of 1 ml per litre of spray (or resin-based agricultural sticker) to freshly prepared 1% Bordeaux mixture immediately before spraying to improve retention, spreading, and rainfastness.

Agronomic Note on Rainfastness / Drying Hours: ICAR-CPCRI publications do not define a fixed agronomic threshold for minimum dry hours required after spray application. Field practitioners recommend allowing sufficient dry time for the copper-lime film to set and dry on the fruit surface before rainfall resumes.

4. Cultural Management and Bunch Covering

The cultural practice of covering of arecanut bunches with polythene covers (125 gauge thickness, measuring 75 x 60 cm) has been found to be very effective in checking the disease by acting as a mechanical barrier against rain splash.

The polythene covers should be tied securely around the stalk/peduncle of the developing bunches before the onset of heavy monsoon rains. This physical covering prevents raindrops carrying Phytophthora sporangia from directly contacting the tender nuts.

Phytosanitary Sanitation: Regularly collect and destroy all fallen, rotted, and mummified nuts from the plantation floor and palm crown before the onset of monsoon showers to eradicate primary inoculum foci.
