Standard Technical Guide: Preparation, Neutrality Testing, and Safe Handling of Bordeaux Mixture (1%)

Source URL: https://icar.org.in/guidelines/bordeaux_mixture_preparation.pdf

Section: Fungicide Formulation Guidelines for Plantation and Horticultural Crops

1. Ingredients and Formulation

Bordeaux mixture is an inorganic copper-based protective fungicide prepared by combining copper sulphate pentahydrate and slaked lime [calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2]. It is NOT formulated with calcium carbonate (CaCO3).

For preparing 100 litres of 1% neutral Bordeaux mixture:

  • Copper Sulphate (CuSO4·5H2O): 1.0 kg (high purity, powdered or dissolved in 50 L water).
  • Slaked Lime [Ca(OH)2] / Quicklime: 1.0 kg (good quality quicklime slaked with water or fresh chemical-grade calcium hydroxide suspended in 50 L water).
  • Water: 100 litres total volume.

2. Preparation Procedure

Dissolve 1 kg copper sulphate in 50 litres of water in a wooden, earthenware, or plastic container (never use iron or metallic vessels, as copper precipitates and corrodes iron). In a separate container, suspend 1 kg slaked lime in 50 litres of water to form a uniform milk of lime.

Pour the copper sulphate solution slowly into the milk of lime suspension while continuously stirring with a wooden stick. Never pour lime into the copper solution.

3. Testing for Neutrality and Acidity

Freshly prepared Bordeaux mixture must be alkaline or neutral (pH 7.0). Excess free copper is phytotoxic and scorches foliage and tender fruit tissues.

  • Iron Blade Test: Dip a clean, bright iron knife or nail into the prepared mixture for one minute. If a reddish-brown copper deposit forms on the metal surface, free copper sulphate is present (acidic). Add more milk of lime until no copper is deposited on the blade.
  • Litmus Paper Test: Red litmus paper dipped in neutral/alkaline Bordeaux mixture should turn blue.

Always apply Bordeaux mixture fresh on the day of preparation; its fungicidal gelatinous precipitate deteriorates upon prolonged standing.

4. Safety and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Applicators must wear protective clothing including masks, safety goggles, and rubber gloves to prevent contact with skin and eyes. Avoid spraying during the hot midday hours (above 32°C) or under high wind conditions exceeding 15 km/h to minimize spray drift and chemical waste.
